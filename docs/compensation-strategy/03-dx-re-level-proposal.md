## 1. Position in five lines

1. The seat was contracted as a product designer on PC and SDM systems. It now also carries the DX Studio website, a sizzle reel, collateral, recurring CMS implementation, motion requests, QA and dev support, and component-refresh pitching on Loblaw properties. Same rate since January 2025.
2. Insight Global's own Toronto listing for a Senior Product Designer contract sits at $41–51/hr. Ibra's $46 is mid-band for the agency's title. A re-level inside that title tops out near $51.
3. Comparable Toronto contract seats through other channels post $48–76 (Motion Recruitment) and $79–91 (Robert Half). The $80–100 belief is partly supported, but not through this agency or this title.
4. Loblaw Digital's own Senior Product Designer evidence is CA$100–132K base, about $142K total. The $180–200K floor is 35–60% above that band. At Loblaw it implies a title above Senior, for which no band was found.
5. Therefore the DX re-level's realistic job is to lift the floor to $55–60, formalise the senior scope and title, and set a review date. It is not the route to the single-engagement goal. That route is a different channel or a different title.

## 7. Discovery conversation with the Loblaw manager (before any number)

Order follows the value conversation: future state, metric, value at stake, pricing guidance. Take notes visibly. Ask one question, then wait.

1. **Future state.** "Over the next twelve months, what does DX Studio need to be true that isn't true today? Of the site, the CMS cadence, the motion work and the component refreshes, which matters most to you?"
2. **Metric.** "How would you know it worked? Pages shipped without an outside studio, turnaround on requests, QA defects, how many PC and SDM teams adopt the components?"
3. **Value at stake.** "If the site and CMS cadence stalled for a quarter, who picks it up and what does that cost?" (This surfaces the outside-studio alternative. Let the manager say the number.)
4. **Pricing guidance, from the top.** "Since January the seat has grown into a senior product and systems role. Comparable contract seats in Toronto post between sixty and seventy-six. I want the requisition to match the role as it runs. What's the path on your side: an amended requisition, or a rate change through Insight Global?"

Then stop. If the manager answers with a budget number, write it down and ask what it is based on. Do not negotiate against it in this meeting.

Close hypothetically: "If you saw a one-page scope with the three options and a start date, is that something you could take forward this month? What else would give you pause?"

## 8. Conversation with the Insight Global recruiter

Money first, then silence.

> "Since January the scope at DX Studio has grown from PC and SDM systems to the DX Studio site, CMS implementation, motion and the component-refresh work. I've discussed it with [manager]. The role as it runs is a senior product and systems seat. I'm asking for sixty an hour with the scope written into the SOW, and I've put a seventy-two option on the table for a twelve-month term with full ownership. What's the bill-rate ceiling on this requisition?"

Then wait.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Budget is fixed mid-contract." | "Understood. Then we fix it at renewal. Until then I'll keep the contracted scope and quote the rest separately. What's the renewal date?" |
| "Others on the team are at forty-six." | "I'm not pricing the team. I'm pricing this seat as it runs." |
| "We'd need a new requisition." | "Good. What does it need to say? I'll send the scope page today." |
| "Can you do fifty?" | "I won't drop the number because you asked. If the term goes to twelve months, I can do fifty-eight." |
| "Why does it cost this much?" | "That's the rate for that scope. Which scope do you want?" |
| "Let us think about it." | "What would need to be true for a yes? Can you decide by [date]?" A hesitant yes is a no. |
| "We can't go above fifty-one for this title." | "Then the title is wrong for the scope. Which title carries the band we're discussing?" |
